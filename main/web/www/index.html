                Network

                Network changes take effect at once (the MAC address after a restart) and have to be confirmed within 120 s, otherwise the previous settings come back. Save them after the confirmation.

                GNSS and PPS

                When the PPS is lost

                The PPS counts as lost after 3.5 s without a pulse. The settings below take effect at once with Apply. When the PPS and the GNSS are back, the server returns to stratum 1 by itself.

                Off: after the holdover the server does not answer at all, so the clients switch to their other servers at once instead of getting stratum 16 answers.

                Relying on time that is no longer synchronised to the GNSS is probably not a good idea: its error grows by an amount nobody measures. Use a stratum below 16 and a long answering time only when the clients absolutely need a time source and have no other one.

                Report contents

                Every report has the status, uptime, satellites and NTP request counts. Additionally:

                Report queue on a TF card (QoS 1 and 2)

                Without a usable TF card (FAT32) only the last 4 reports are queued, in RAM, with at most 50 clients each: they are lost at a power failure or crash, and the oldest report is dropped first. Flash is not used, so it does not wear out. With a TF card the reports are queued on the card (an empty 16 GB card can hold more than 36,000 reports) with up to the client limit below; when the card is full, the oldest reports are dropped first.

                Firmware Update

                A new firmware can be installed over the network (Over The Ethernet, OTE). First the device has to accept updates:

                • Once: press Restart with updates enabled under Upload. The device restarts and accepts updates until its next restart (the one after an upload included); after that they are off again. Log in again after about a minute.
                • Permanently: tick Accept firmware updates over the network, press Save Settings and then Reboot.

                Then the image can be sent over HTTPS (TCP 443) in two ways:

                1. With this page: choose the image under Upload and press Upload and restart. Your login to this page authorises the upload; the OTE password is not used.
                2. With the script on a PC: python tools/ote_upload.py -i <mDNS host name>.local -f build/ESP32TimeServer.bin --fingerprint <certificate SHA-256> (the fingerprint is shown under Status). The script authorises the upload with the OTE password, which it takes from the environment variable OTE_PASSWORD or asks for; no login is needed.

                Which file: ESP32TimeServer.bin from the build folder, built with the profile for this board's chip revision (), not the merged flash image. The device refuses images of another project or chip revision.

                What happens: the device writes the image into its spare firmware slot, checks the complete image and only then selects it for the next start; until then the running firmware stays untouched. It restarts 5 s after the upload: reload this page after about a minute. During the upload (at most 5 min) this page pauses its other requests.

                Settings: mDNS host name: the device announces itself as <name>.local, also while updates are off (letters, digits and '-'). OTE password: at least 8 characters while updates are on; an empty field keeps the stored password. Changes take effect with Apply, which restarts the device once (a new OTE password only counts from then on), and are kept with Save Settings.

                Security: the image is not signed, so anyone who can log in to this page or knows the OTE password can install any firmware. Use long random passwords and switch updates off when you do not need them. Wrong OTE passwords count towards the login lock-out, and the allow-list under Security applies to both ways.
